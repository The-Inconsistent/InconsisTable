# House decisions

Where a rulebook leaves a mechanic open and the engine has to pick a behaviour, the choice is written here, never made
silently in code. Each entry names the mechanic, the books or rulesets it affects, the options, the choice and why.
A ruleset's own decisions live in its manifest; a table's live in its `house_rule` rows.

| # | Mechanic | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who rolls | The app never rolls a Test unless the ruleset opts in; players roll physical dice and report. It may roll on a random table when the GM asks. | Carried over from Auspex: the table keeps its dice. |
| 2 | Initiative ties (`initiative.ranked`) | After the score and the manifest's tiebreaks, a tie goes by actor id order. | Deterministic, so every seat shows the same order; a ruleset that wants a roll-off declares it as a tiebreak. |
| 3 | Removing the actor whose turn it is | The turn passes to the next in order; if they were last, a new round starts. | The table never stalls on a turn nobody holds. |
| 4 | `rerollEachRound` | At the end of a round the order clears and nobody is current; the GM starts the next round, which ranks again. | Rolling is the table's, with physical dice; the app waits for the scores. |
| 5 | Damage past a track's minimum (`health.tracks`) | It is lost. | No ruleset in scope carries overflow on the same track; one that does declares an overflow track. |
| 6 | Dropping to a track's minimum | Starts "dying" only when that track declares death saves. | Tracks without death saves (Shock-like) mean nothing at their floor by themselves. |
| 7 | Damage to an actor already dying | Changes nothing by itself; no failed save is added. | Books differ (5e adds a failure); a ruleset adds it explicitly (`deathSaves.failuresOnDamage`) instead of the engine guessing. |
| 8 | Healing above a lowered max | Healing never lowers a value above a max that has since dropped. | A heal must never hurt. |
| 9 | Healing the dead | Refused; undoing death is a GM edit to the field. | Death is a story beat, not a counter. |
| 10 | Condition stacks and effects | An effect applies once per active condition, whatever its stack count. `ponytail:` a ruleset with stacking effects needs a `perStack` flag. | No ruleset in P1 stacks an effect's value. |
| 11 | Co-GM view in the engine (replaced by #31) | A co-GM sees what a GM sees in actor values; their scopes limit actions and the codex. `ponytail:` per-scope actor views if a table needs a combat-only co-GM blind to secrets. | Running combat needs the stat blocks. |
| 12 | Damage to a stable actor (`health.tracks`) | Any damage that reaches the track starts "dying" again, with both death-save tallies reset to 0. | A stable actor sits at the track's minimum, so a hit is the same as dropping to it; a ruleset that keeps the tallies, or adds a failure as in #7, declares it explicitly. |
| 13 | Instant death (`health.tracks`, `instantDeathOverflow`) | Overflow is the damage left once the track is at its minimum, after temporary points. The actor dies when overflow is **at least** the value of the track's `instantDeathOverflow` expression, whether it was healthy or already dying. A book that says "exceeds" writes `max + 1`. | Books phrase the threshold differently; the manifest's number decides, the engine only compares. A ruleset without the parameter has no instant death. |
| 14 | Counted expiry (`conditions.expire`) | A condition row counts down at the end of each of its holder's own turns (`ownTurnEnd`) or at each round end (`roundEnd`) and goes when the last tick is used; no count (`expiresAfter` null) is one tick. The turn a condition is applied on counts as a tick: "until the end of its next turn" applied during its own turn is entered as 2. `encounterEnd` ignores the count and `manual` never expires. `ponytail:` an expiry tied to the applier's turn needs the applier on the row. | The book's wording ("end of its next turn", "start of the caster's turn") varies; the table sets the count when it applies the condition, the engine does not guess. |
| 15 | Order at a turn change | The caller runs `initiative.ranked` `next`, then `conditions.expire` `turnEnd` for the actor whose turn just ended, then `roundEnd` if the round wrapped. Holders are visited in initiative order, then by id. At `encounter.end`: `conditions.expire` `encounterEnd`, then `rest` `encounterEnd`. | One order for every seat and server; delta order is the undo order. |
| 16 | Rest events (`rest`) | An event resets only the resources whose `resetOn` lists that event. A long rest does not run the short-rest resets, so a resource that restores on both lists both. A reset value outside a resource's bounds is held inside them. | Books differ on whether a long rest includes a short one; the manifest says it per resource. |
| 17 | Pack cites and long text | A pack entry needs a `cite` unless the pack's license is `original` (the author's own work) or the ruleset is homebrew. In a `private` pack, a `text` field value over 280 characters is a warning, never a refusal. `srd`, `cc-by` and `orc` packs must name an attribution. | Pack licenses have no `homebrew`; `original` is its analogue. The warning nudges a summary in the importer's own words (principle 2). |
| 18 | Creation answers | A `choose` step takes exactly its `count` of distinct picks, and `copy` only with `count` 1. `assign` offers a manifest table (each value once) or a point buy; a score rolled on real dice is a `fields` step on the int field, bounded by its min and max. No field has two writers. A skipped step needs `optional`. | The builder never invents a method; a ruleset that rolls its scores says so in its field bounds and `decisions[]`. |
| 19 | Re-applying a condition | `refresh` restarts the duration: the count is the one given, and null when none is given (a manifest declares no default count yet, so there is nothing else to restart from). `none` is a no-op. `stack` adds a stack up to `maxStacks`. | The books say "the duration restarts", never "extends"; one rule for every ruleset. |
| 20 | Reporting a called Test (`test.report`) | The player of a called character reports the physical roll once; that report stands. Only the GM side (the GM, a co-GM with the session scope) reports for anyone and may replace a report while the call is open. The server resolves the outcome with `dice.sumVsTarget`, from the target and modifier computed for the character when the call was made. | A player who could report again could retake a roll that failed once the outcome is shown. The numbers are kept at call time so a condition applied meanwhile does not change what the phone was told. |
| 21 | Who sees a called Test's numbers and results | A called player sees only the calls naming a character they hold, and in them only their own character. The target number and its inputs are sent only if the GM did not hide them **and** the ruleset lets the owner see what the target is computed from (`CompiledTest.targetVisibility`); the modifier and the total likewise (`modifierVisibility`). Their own outcome (success, failure, critical) is sent when the target is shown, or when the GM chose to tell them with a hidden one. Closing with `reveal` also shows the other called characters' outcome and die, never their modifier, total or the target. The table screen sees only that a Test is open and how many have reported. A hidden target never leaves a GM seat. | Conservative: knowing pass or fail narrows a hidden target, so the GM opts in; a derived number is as hidden as its most hidden input. A ruleset where players see each other's rolls can reveal on close. |
| 22 | Building a character (`actor.build`) | A character is built once: it counts as built as soon as it has any field or choice row, and a second build is refused ("Already built"); the GM corrects values with `field.set`. An untyped character is built as the only entity the ruleset's `creation` builds (more than one: the GM creates it with its type). The `name` field, when a step sets it, also becomes the actor's name. Values the creation decided skip `editableBy` (a player writes their own build even into a `gm` field); their types and ranges still apply. The creation's picks are stored as `actor_choice` rows, outside the delta log, so undo does not remove them. | Re-running a build over existing values would silently overwrite the GM's corrections. The creation is the ruleset's own, checked by the server, so the field's later `editableBy` is not the builder's concern. |
| 23 | Starting resources | When a character or creature is first written, each resource it carries starts at its ceiling when it has one, otherwise at its floor. A resource whose bounds cannot be computed yet (a field it reads is still unset) is not created; the GM sets that field and the resource then. | Books say a character starts at full hit points and with full uses; a resource with no ceiling (spare points, a counter) starts empty. |
| 24 | Creatures from a pack (`actor.spawn`) | Each creature is a new `creature` actor named "<entry name> N", counting on from the highest number already used with that exact name (never reusing a freed number's gap). Its values are the entry body copied as rows, the `name` field included as written; the pack version is immutable, so the rows are the snapshot and later edits are the creature's own. The entry must be in a pack attached to the room and served to it (not taken down, and a `private` pack only while its owner is a GM here). `temporary` is kept on the actor for the encounter's end. Undoing a spawn or a build reverts the logged values only: the spawned actors and a build's choice rows stay. | Numbering from the highest keeps "Goblin 3" unambiguous when "Goblin 2" has been killed and removed. |
| 25 | Sheet layout and the Breakdown of a number | A sheet reads `layout[<entity id>]`, else `layout.sheet`, else lists derived values, resources, then fields. A `show` path is a field id, or `derived.<id>` / `res.<id>`; a bare id is a field, else a derived value, else a resource. A path the seat holds no value for is left out. The Breakdown of a derived number is its base (the server's total minus the visible conditions' shares) plus one line per condition the seat may see; a condition it may not see is folded into the base, never named. A condition's share is the value with it minus the value without it. | The manifest does not say which key a layout hangs on. Folding keeps the lines adding up to the number the server sent without telling a player a hidden effect exists. |
| 26 | End of an encounter (`encounter.end`) | `conditions.expire` `encounterEnd` and `rest` `encounterEnd` run as one logged action for every actor that stays; then each combatant that is a `temporary` creature and not marked `keep` is deleted with its rows. The deletes are not undoable: Undo skips the steps of a deleted actor and notes them, and skips the round and turn steps of an ended encounter. | A creature spawned for one fight has no life after it, and an undo that could resurrect rows would need a copy of every one. | 
| 27 | Running the order (combat tracker) | The GM types every initiative; the order is highest first, combatants with none last, a tie keeping the place it came in, and the GM settles a tie by moving one place past an equal (never past a different score). `turn.prev` only moves the marker (and the round back from the top); expiries already ticked come back by Undo. When the combatant whose turn it is leaves, the turn passes to whoever follows, or to nobody if they were last. A creature spawned during a fight joins it as a `foe`, hidden. A ruleset that re-rolls initiative every round is refused by `turn.next` until the tracker can take new scores mid-wrap. | The books give the score, not the tie; the table settles it, and the app never rolls. Hidden by default so a spawn never spoils an ambush. |
| 28 | Encounters across sessions | An encounter may stay open across sessions; with no session running it is paused. Every `encounter.*` action except `encounter.start` (which still says "Start a session first"), every `turn.*` action and the turn-driven `rule.op` ops (`initiative.ranked`, `conditions.expire`) are refused with "Start a session to continue", `encounter.end` included. The snapshot carries `encounter.paused`; the TV and phones show the order marked Paused, with no turn banner and no vibration. Undo stays within the running session. Undoing a turn pass whose previous holder has left the fight skips that step and notes it (`gone`). | The books say nothing about a fight that spans two sittings; the order, rounds and conditions should survive the break without anything ticking while the table is away. |
| 29 | Dice pool (`dice.poolSuccesses`) | The pool is `pool` plus the call's `modifier`, in dice, held from 0 up to 100 (the engine's dice cap); the difficulty number is the call's `target`. A special die is one of the pool's dice: it scores in the bands like any other and also flags `critical` or `complication` when its face is in that flag's range; a pool smaller than the declared special dice has fewer of them. A pass is `score ≥ DN` (`score > DN` with `ties: beat`). Shifts are the dice of the `shift` band, special die included, that can come off the score with the roll still passing, one band die at a time. A report sorts the ordinary dice into bands (dice in no band are left out) and names the special dice's faces; it may count fewer dice than the pool but never more. Bands may not overlap, and a face range must fit the die. The manifest shape is `bands[]` and `special{}` (not the `faces` map sketched in design §4.2): a range with a worth says more than a face map for a die with many faces. | The books say how a face scores and what a special die flags, not how a Test Call's modifier or a smaller pool treats it. Counting a special die in the bands keeps the score what the players add up at the table. |
| 30 | What a player sees of a Dice pool | The pool size follows the modifier's visibility (it is told as one number). A player's own flags are always told: they come from the dice the player read out. Pass or fail follows `showOutcome` as for a sum Test. The margin and the shifts give the difficulty number away (score minus margin), so they are sent only when the number is shown or the GM closes with `reveal`, and then also for the other called characters. Closing with `reveal` therefore reveals the difficulty number to a player who knows their own score, though `target` itself is still not sent. | Conservative like #21: a hidden number is not worked out from the margin. A table that wants players to learn it reveals the Test when it closes. |
| 31 | What a co-GM sees and writes (replaces #11) | A co-GM sees and writes hidden parts only where one of their scopes covers them. `combat`: hidden Combatants, the values of Creatures and NPCs, the whole Encounter, `gm` pack entries. `session`: hidden Test targets and inputs (a hidden difficulty number and a Dice pool's hidden fields, as in #30) and the session notes, which the same scope may edit. `codex.secrets`: secret codex entries and the notes on them. `party`: the `gm` fields and resources of Characters, and the invites. `codex`: every codex entry, `gm` ones included, with each entry's whole audience (so a co-GM's own new entry stays in their view and saving it never drops other readers); the notes on entries still need `codex.secrets`. A co-GM with no scope sees as a player who holds nothing, plus their own held Characters. The GM, the room's owner and an admin who opened the room without a seat see everything; an admin who holds a seat sees by that seat. A `gm` value is written under the same scope that shows it: `party` for a Character's, `combat` for a Creature's or NPC's. Values everyone can see (`public` or `party` visibility) a co-GM may write on any actor, as far as their action permission goes: a combat co-GM may damage a PC's public HP but never touches that PC's `gm` values. A Combatant's health numbers follow the same rule as the actor's values, so the Encounter and the sheet never disagree. The manifest is read whole by a co-GM with `party` or `combat` (`ponytail:` per-kind definitions if a ruleset needs them kept). | A combat-only co-GM can run Creatures without reading codex secrets or hidden Test targets; #11 gave every co-GM the GM's sight, so the scopes only limited actions. |
| 32 | No accounts until a provider is chosen | Every browser is an anonymous user: an `it_device` cookie (random secret, minted by the web proxy, only its hash stored in `device`) names a `app_user` made on first sight, and every such user is a `creator`. Admin is the shared `ADMIN_KEY` (header `x-admin-key`, or the socket `adminKey`), not a user. The deployment is local or LAN only (`docs/deploy.md`). | A lost cookie is a lost identity and one key is all admin there is, which a private table can live with; neither belongs on a public host. A provider later replaces `deviceUser` and the key, and the seat rules stay as they are (`ponytail:` in `packages/auth`). |
| 33 | Accounts through our own identity provider (replaces #32 once shipped) | An OpenID Provider (`oidc-provider`) in its own private repository, with its own server-rendered pages (ADR 0006), at `auth.<domain>`; the api is its confidential client and keeps its own opaque sessions (30 days idle, 90 days at most; admin 1 hour idle, 24 hours at most). Only GMs and room owners need an account; players stay anonymous through invites and QR. Sign-in is a passkey plus ten single-use recovery codes, no email; an admin may issue new recovery codes after checking in person, logged. Accounts exist only on HTTPS deployments. The first admin is granted by a CLI on the server; admins sign in with a user-verified passkey, never a recovery code. Clients are registered statically and our own skip consent. Deleting an account needs every room with other users handed over first. | A second app needs single sign-on, and the user store stays ours at no cost. ADR 0005. |
| 34 | Runtime of the identity provider | Node 22 LTS, not Bun; Bun stays the package manager and test runner. | `oidc-provider` warns "Unsupported runtime" on Bun, and the identity service must run where its library is supported. |
| 35 | Auth outside the single mutation path | Signing in, signing out, back-channel logout and managing one's own passkeys and recovery codes are REST writes, added to the exceptions of principle 6. | OIDC is redirects and HTTP endpoints by design; none of these writes touches campaign state. |
| 36 | Seated order (`initiative.seated`) | The order is the seat order the table sets when the game starts, kept in `combatant.ord`; nothing is scored. The first player is a logged turn value (`firstId`), so Undo takes it back. `fixed`: every round starts at the first seat. `rotate`: at each round start the first player moves one place along the order. `manual`: a seat that runs the table sets it (`turn.first`). A combatant who joins mid-game takes the place the table gives it. One who leaves is skipped; if it was first, first passes to the one after it, and if it was acting, as #3. | Rulebooks give the direction of play and how the first-player marker moves, not what happens when a seat leaves. Logging the first player keeps Undo exact, as the order itself is never logged. |
| 37 | Phases (`phases`) | A round runs the manifest's phases in order. In a `turns` phase each combatant acts once, in order from the first player; after the last, the next phase starts again from the first player. A `simultaneous` phase has no current turn: each seat marks its own characters done, and the phase moves on when every combatant in the order is done. A seat that runs the table may move on without waiting, and the log names who was not done. `turn.prev` stops at the first turn of a phase and anywhere in a simultaneous one ("Use Undo to go back a phase"). After the last phase the round ends. `ownTurnEnd` expiries tick only at the end of a turn in a `turns` phase; `roundEnd` ticks as always. At a phase change: `turnEnd` for the actor whose turn ended, then `roundEnd` if the round wrapped, as #15. | Books name the phases but not how a shared phase closes when a player is slow or away. A game with only simultaneous phases counts its expiries by round, so nothing ticks twice. |
| 38 | End of a game (`scoring.endWhen`) | After every action the server evaluates each `endWhen` and tells every seat which are met. It never ends the game itself: a seat that runs the table ends it with `game.end`, which may come before any trigger is met (the table stops early, and the result says so). Ending closes the game's encounter as #26 does and needs a running session, as #28. It is not undoable; a score is corrected afterwards with `result.set`, which ranks again, logged. Which triggers are met is told to every seat, even when a trigger reads a hidden value; a ruleset keeps a trigger to itself by not declaring it. | Rulebooks differ on whether a game ends at once or at the end of the round; the table picks the moment, and the app only says the condition is there. |
| 39 | Final score and ranking (`scoring`) | At `game.end` every combatant is scored with `score`, an automa or NPC included. Ranked by score in `order`, then each tiebreak in its own order. Still tied after every tiebreak: they share the place, and the next place is skipped (1, 1, 3). A cooperative game (`scoring.mode: coop`) is not ranked: each `endWhen` names an outcome (`win`, `lose`) and the whole table gets it; ended early, it is `lose`. | A shared win is a printed outcome in many rulebooks, so a tie is not broken by actor id as an order is (#2). |
| 40 | Hidden scores at the end | While the game runs, a running total is as hidden as its most hidden input, as #21: one built from an `owner` value reaches only that owner and the seats that see everything. At `game.end` every seat is sent each combatant's total, place and tiebreak values; the values the total is built from keep their visibility. | The totals are what the table compares at the end; turning over a hidden objective is done at the table, with the physical card. |
| 41 | Reported Tests (`kind: reported`) | A Test the table rolls and works out itself: the manifest lists 2 to 8 outcomes, each `{ id, label, cite }`, best first (a homebrew ruleset may leave the cites out, as for every rule entry). The GM calls it naming the characters, with no target, dice or modifier. Who may report follows #20: the character's player once, the GM side for anyone and to correct. Who sees the result follows #21: a player sees their own outcome, the others' only once the GM closes with `reveal`, the table screen only how many have reported; with no number to hide, the result is always told to the called player. The server refuses an outcome id the Test does not list ("That outcome is not one this Test lists."), a dice report on a reported Test and an outcome on a dice Test, and records the id; it does no arithmetic and the app never rolls it. | The dice and the working-out stay on the table (principle 5). Most story games (three result bands, partial success, levels of success) then run without a primitive each. |
| 42 | Degrees of success (`degrees` on a `sumVsTarget` Test) | A `sumVsTarget` Test may declare `degrees: { step: 1..20, naturalShift?: boolean, cite }`. With it the outcome is one of `criticalSuccess`, `success`, `failure`, `criticalFailure`: total at least target + step is `criticalSuccess`; total at least the target is `success`; total at most target − step is `criticalFailure`; anything else is `failure`. With `naturalShift`, a natural highest face of the single die (the kept die, with advantage) moves the result one level up and a natural 1 one level down, never past the four levels; it needs a 1dX Test and a reported natural. Ties follow the Test's own `ties` rule at the success/failure line. `degrees` replaces `critOn` (a manifest with both is refused), is a rule entry that needs a cite unless the ruleset is homebrew, and without it the Test is exactly as before (`success`, `failure`, `critical`). The server computes it; the player still reports the dice. Also: `initiative` is optional in a manifest. A ruleset without it has no turn order, `encounter.start` is refused ("This ruleset has no turn order.") and the TRACK tab says so instead of showing the order controls. | Four-level systems (beat the DC by 10 or roll a natural 20) are common, and the margin is arithmetic the server already does (ADR 0004). A game with no turn order (a pure story or one-shot-roll game) should import without a fake order. Other dice mechanics (roll-under, highest of a pool, fate dice) are not part of this. |
| 43 | Spend requests (`request.*`) | A player may ask to spend 1 to 99 from a resource of a character they hold, with a note of up to 140 characters, while a session runs. Never from a `gm`-visibility resource, a `gm` or `campaign` pool ("Players can't spend from that."), a health track (it takes damage through its own op), or a resource they may already spend themselves (`editableBy` `owner` or `ownerSpendOnly`: "You can spend that yourself."). At most 5 pending per user ("You have too many requests waiting."). The GM side (`request:resolve`; a co-GM with the `session` scope) approves or denies, with an optional reason. Approval applies `-amount` through the same op and pipeline as a GM's own resource change, so the server clamps it and the log keeps it for `event.undo`; a spend that no longer applies refuses and the request stays to be denied. A request answered, cancelled by its asker or already answered refuses a second answer ("That request was already answered."). Pending requests are cancelled when the session ends or the game is archived. Seats get the pending requests (the GM side all, a player their own, a screen none) and a player their own answers from the last 10 minutes, so the phone can say what the GM answered. | Players can still ask for what the rules keep with the GM without a chat channel, and the spend stays one logged, clamped change (principle 6). Not built yet: a request from a shared party pool (the table allows a NULL `actor_id`; the request is refused until pools have storage, `ponytail:` in `internal/request`), and in a GM-less room a request will apply directly and be logged (group D). |
| 44 | GM-less permissions (`table_mode` `gmless` and `solo`) | With no GM seat, every player seat (a member, or a guest's token) holds `session:run`, `encounter:run`, `clock:run`, `test:call`, `codex:write` and `codex:reveal`, and nobody holds `codex:read_secret`, `character:edit:any` or `request:resolve`. The owner (still `campaign.owner_user_id`, now a player seat) also keeps the room: `campaign:update`, `campaign:archive`, `member:invite`, `member:remove`, `token:issue`, `character:create` and the owner-only ones, and a character the owner creates is theirs to play. A Test is called by any seat for characters only, its target always shown (a call with `hideTarget`, or whose ruleset hides the target, is refused); the permission to call is not the permission to report for another seat's character, and `phase.done` and `test.report` no longer fall back to the table permissions. Nothing is GM-only: no seat receives a `gm` codex entry, GM notes, session notes, a `gm` pack entry or a `gm` clock, a new entry is `party`, revealing to `gm` is refused, and a clock made `gm` is the party's. The whole Encounter and every Test call are shared, and a Creature's or NPC's hidden values are NOT: every seat reads them as a player does. Undo takes back a step of one's own (the seat that logged it, `session_event.seat_user_id`); the owner takes back any; a guest has no user to match and undoes none. A solo room seats one: the owner's seat is the one, so a stranger's accept is refused. Switching to `gmless` or `solo` turns every GM and co-GM into a player and revokes open invites to a GM seat; switching back makes the owner the GM. A new owner (a transfer) and an admin's seat sit as players while there is no GM. | A GM-less table has to run itself, and what would be kept from the players has no one to keep it: nothing is secret rather than secret from everyone but the owner (p2-plan decisions 1 to 3). The sight is not read from the permissions, which would hand every player the GM side; it is the player's, plus the shared fight and calls. The seat view reads the mode from the campaign row loaded with the snapshot, and an action reads it from the row in its own transaction, so a socket opened before the switch keeps nothing. Added after review: creatures and NPCs prepared under a GM become visible to the table after the switch (names and public fields; `gm` values stay hidden); `actor.spawn` of a `gm` entry is refused as `entryNotInPack`; a Test without a GM is refused when its target or modifier reads an `owner` or `gm` field (`testTableOnly`); a solo room issues no guest token; only the owner announces a book source; a player's first report stands, the owner corrects. Not decided, and left as it is: rotating the person who narrates, and a hidden Test target for a table that wants one (both would be new options). |
| 45 | Freeform | A homebrew ruleset (`id` `freeform`, every source `homebrew`, so no cites) with only the engine's structure and no game's numbers or outcome names: a character with a name and notes, a `Health` track (ceiling 10, uncapped so the GM raises one character past it by hand: a field cannot default, and a missing one blocks the sheet), and one reported Test `check` with outcomes `Success`, `Partial`, `Failure`; turns go round the table (`initiative.seated`, the first player rotating), so a made-up game can still run a fight. `game` is `Freeform`, 1 to 12 players, all three modes. The api does not serve it: the web holds the manifest as a constant (`apps/web/lib/freeform`) and "Use Freeform" (Choose game or Quick game) imports it into the user's own library through the ordinary owner-scoped `POST /api/rulesets`, once (the same manifest answers `unchanged`; a library that already has `freeform` is reused). It ships no content, since the three words and a ten are the engine's own scaffolding, not a book's (ADR 0001). | Design §3.3, p2-plan group E |
| 46 | Ruleset templates | The library's import panel offers "Start from a template" for five families, named generically (never a trademark): "d20 roll vs a number" (`sumVsTarget`, the number and the modifier supplied when the Test is called), "2d6 bands" and "Clocks and position" (a reported Test with three placeholder outcomes; the story's clocks are the room's, not the ruleset's), "Dice pool counting hits" (`poolSuccesses`) and "Percentile" (a reported Test with two outcomes: roll-under is not an engine primitive, the table reads the dice and reports). A template holds only engine structure: one entity, one `Resource 1` with no ceiling, and the Test. No number, outcome name or entry name of any game: the names are `Outcome A/B/C` and `Resource 1`, the only numbers are the dice shape the engine needs (`1d20`, one `d6` scoring on its top face). Every `cite` is `""` and the source's `license` is `""`, so the validator's problem list is the checklist of what the user must fill in (a cite and a license, and the placeholders to rename) and the template can never pass for a rule taken from a book.
